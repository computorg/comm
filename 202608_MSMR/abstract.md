# MSMR 2026

## Computo

**Authors** Julien Chiquet and Pierre Neuvial, on behalf of the Computo Editorial Board (Mathieu Carrière, Marie-Pierre Étienne, Aymeric Stamm and Nelle Varoquaux)

**Contact** contact@computo-journal.org

**Title** Computo: Redefining Academic Publishing through Native Computational Reproducibility

**Abstract**

In the current landscape of statistics and machine learning, the traditional "static" PDF publication format often fails to bridge the gap between theoretical claims and computational reality. As the reproducibility crisis highlights, reconstructing the exact numerical path from data to results remains a major hurdle for researchers. This presentation introduces [Computo](https://computo-journal.org/), a [diamond open-access journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_open_access) published by the French Statistical Society (SFdS), specifically designed to address these challenges by promoting reproducible research at its core.

*Computo* publishes papers that make a genuine scientific contribution, whether through new methodology, theoretical insight, empirical understanding, or reproducible software. We particularly welcome:
* Methods papers: original stats/ML developments or numerical studies illustrating theory;
* Case studies & surveys: applications to specific data questions, including neutral comparison studies;
* Software & tutorials: package presentations grounded in concrete use cases and benchmarking.

*Computo* moves beyond the static paper by leveraging modern literate programming tools, combining the use of notebooks, virtual environment management, and continuous integration. Every submission to the journal is required to be a fully reproducible document, where text, equations, and code are inextricably linked. This technical infrastructure ensures that all figures, tables, and benchmarks can be regenerated from source with a single command, providing full transparency of the methodological implementation. While templates are currently provided for _Python_, _R_, and _Julia_ users, the underlying workflow is language-agnostic and can theoretically support any programming language within the reproducible process.

By integrating these practices into the academic reward system, *Computo* aims to demonstrate that adopting a reproducibility-first editorial policy is not only feasible but essential for the advancement of trustworthy science. This talk will detail our technical architecture, our feedback from the first years of operation, and how such a model can be scaled to other quantitative disciplines to foster a more transparent research ecosystem.
